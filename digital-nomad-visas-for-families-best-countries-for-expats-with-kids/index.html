Understanding Digital Nomad Visas

A digital nomad visa allows remote workers to live in a foreign country while working for an employer or running a business based elsewhere. For families these visas often extend to spouses and children so you can travel the world together. But not all digital nomad visas are created equal and the eligibility criteria can vary greatly from one country to another. Key Points for Families

Before you pack your bags you need to consider a few things:

Eligibility Requirements

Some countries have income thresholds or job type requirements. For example Costa Rica requires a minimum income of $3,000 per month for individuals and $5,000 for families.

Family Inclusion

Not all visas include family members. Make sure to check if your chosen destination allows dependents to join you and under what conditions. For example Portugal’s digital nomad visa allows family members (spouses and dependent children) to join the main applicant. Steps to Apply for a Digital Nomad Visa with Your Family

For you to go on this journey, you have to plan in detail. The following steps will simplify the whole process for you.

Research Your Destination

Requirements for the visa: There are requirements set for each country that provides a visa for digital nomads. This includes the amount of income, employment sectors, and any additional documents. Look at them closely to figure out if you qualify.

Assess Family Requirements: Things like, healthcare, education, security and lifestyle, should be used to guide you in deciding whether you want to relocate as a family. You should do your research on schools, hospitals, and cheap housing so that your family can be safe.

Furthermore, read this article for more information on relocating with kids and family relocation for digital nomads.

Collect Your Required Documentation

Every country will have its own list of documents. However, this is what should cover the general list of the documents category:

  • Each family member has his or her own identification (Passport)
  • Letter of self employment or employment
  • Letter that shows how much you earn (eg. Bank statement or paystub)
  • Insurance for the whole family’s health
  • Children’s birth certificates
  • Marriage certificates other than that not necessary
  • Always check if the documents are up to date since at times there may be a need of a certified translation.

Submit the Application

Depending on the destination, the options available would be to submit an application online or visit the nearest embassy or consulate. Before submitting an application, please verify the application costs, period needed for processing, and application submission procedures.

In case there are dependents of the primary applicant applying with him for the visa application, their documents are also to be attached to the visa application of the main applicant.

Wait for Approval

Do not hurry, patience is a key point Persistence is needed! Depending on the procedures of a specific country, one could wait anywhere from several weeks to a several months. So it is a good idea to make preparations and research about the housing, the schools you wish to send your kids to and various activities in that particular region. Prepare for the Move

Look for housing

It is advised for most expats to start with renting a place for a limited time so that they can familiarize themselves with the area. Such services as Airbnb and Booking.com or any regional website could assist in doing so.

Look for local Schools

There are Private local public institutions, international, and even home school options to choose from depending on where you plan to move. An international school is likely to be one of the most chosen as many of the times it uses a universal curriculum in English.

Plan for Local Essentials

Once you land you would want to go about securing a local mobile plan, a bank in that particular area, and a mode of transport.

Challenges of Moving Abroad with Kids (and How to Overcome Them)

Although it is exciting to be a digital nomad, the prospect of relocating with children implies certain situations that require knowledge on how to navigate. There is particular advice for those situations:

Nurturing Grasp over New Traditions

    All the children can go through a culture shock especially in those countries where the language and the customs are not remotely similar.

    Tip: There are books, videos and even local traditions to them so that the kids can be familiar with the new culture even before the family relocates.

    Asking Questions Regarding Education

      In particular, to locate a suitable school can be a challenge and even impossible where English is foreign language and Isn’t the first one.

      Tip: Fish for international schools well in advance & if need be, rely on online schooling facilities. Seek help from other families living abroad.

      Seeking Out Other Families

        Before, during and after embarking on a new journey, children can often find themselves feeling downright lonely and even abandoned. This is even more so for parents who have to work while childcare in an alien place.

        Tip: There are various groups across Facebook and local communities and meet-ups as well to expand the family’s networks and share advice. This will make the transition much easier for you and your children.

        Making Time for Family

          Adopting a life where work, travel and other responsibilities towards children run hand in hand isn’t easy. In fact, it gets rather difficult if there’s no equilibrium.

          Tip: Develop a family schedule that everyone can stick to. Set aside specific time for work, family fun, and discovering your new place.
