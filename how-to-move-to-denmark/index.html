How to Move to Denmark

December 15, 2024 by admin

Denmark is a small Nordic country with a high standard of living and a strong economy. It is a popular destination for people looking to live and work abroad, but moving to Denmark can also present challenges. In this blog, we will discuss the steps you need to take to move to Denmark, as well as the pros and cons of living in Denmark including how to move without a job, and how to move from the US.

How to move to Denmark

Before you start the process of moving to Denmark, there are a few things you’ll need to consider:

  • Do you need a visa? If you’re from an EU/EEA country, Switzerland, or a Nordic country, you don’t need a visa to live and work in Denmark. If you’re from another country, you’ll need to apply for a residence permit or work permit.
  • Do you speak Danish? While many Danes speak English, it’s a good idea to learn Danish to fully integrate into Danish society.
  • Do you have a job or a place to live? While not necessary, having a job or a place to live in Denmark can make the process of moving much smoother.

Once you’ve sorted out these basics, you can start the process of moving to Denmark:

Step 1: Find a place to live. Denmark has several different options for housing, including rental apartments, co-housing, and student housing. You can use websites like BoligPortal or Lejebolig to search for available housing.

Step 2: Get a CPR number. A CPR number is a unique identification number given to all residents in Denmark. You’ll need this number to access healthcare, open a bank account, and more. You can get a CPR number by visiting your local Citizen Service Center with your passport and rental contract.

Step 3: Register for a health insurance card. All residents of Denmark are required to have a health insurance card, which gives them access to healthcare services. You can register for a health insurance card at your local Citizen Service Center.

Step 4: Open a bank account. You’ll need a Danish bank account to receive your salary, pay bills, and more. Most banks in Denmark require you to have a CPR number and proof of address to open an account.

Step 5: Apply for a residence permit or work permit. If you’re from a country outside of the EU/EEA or Switzerland, you’ll need to apply for a residence permit or work permit. You can do this through the Danish Immigration Service.

How to move to Denmark without a job

Moving to Denmark without a job can be a bit more challenging, but it’s not impossible. Here are some tips for making the move:

  • Consider studying in Denmark. Many universities in Denmark offer programs taught in English and students are allowed to work part-time while studying.
  • Apply for a working holiday visa. If you’re between 18-30 years old, you may be eligible for a working holiday visa, which allows you to work and travel in Denmark for up to one year.
  • Look for jobs before you move. While it’s not necessary to have a job before you move, it can make the process much easier. You can use job search websites like Jobindex or Graduateland to search for available jobs in Denmark.

Moving to Denmark from the EU

If you’re an EU citizen, you have the right to live and work in Denmark without a residence or work permit. However, you still need to register with the Danish authorities and obtain a CPR number.

To move to Denmark from an EU country, follow these steps:

  • Register your residence: Within three months of your arrival in Denmark, you need to register your residence with the local authorities. You will need to bring your passport or ID card, a tenancy agreement or proof of address, and evidence of your EU citizenship.
  • Obtain a CPR number: After registering your residence, you can apply for a CPR number. You will need to visit the local citizen service center and provide your passport or ID card, proof of address, and evidence of your EU citizenship.
  • Look for work: Once you have your CPR number, you can start looking for work in Denmark. You can search for job openings online or through local recruitment agencies.
  • Get health insurance: As an EU citizen, you are entitled to free or low-cost healthcare in Denmark. You can apply for health insurance through the national health service.

Frequently Asked Questions

  1. Do I need to learn Danish to move to Denmark?

While learning Danish is not mandatory to move to Denmark, it can certainly help with integrating into Danish society and finding a job.

  1. How much money do I need to move to Denmark?

The amount of money needed to move to Denmark can vary depending on various factors such as accommodation, transportation, and other living expenses. It is recommended to have at least 50,000 DKK ($8,000) to cover initial expenses.

  1. Is it difficult to find a job in Denmark as a foreigner?

It can be challenging to find a job in Denmark as a foreigner, especially if you do not have a specific skill set. However, certain industries, such as IT and healthcare, are in high demand of foreign workers.

  1. Can I bring my pet with me when moving to Denmark?

Yes, pets can be brought into Denmark but are subject to certain rules and regulations, such as being vaccinated and having a pet passport.

  1. Can I apply for permanent residency in Denmark?

Yes, permanent residency is available for those who have been living and working in Denmark for a certain period of time. The requirements for permanent residency can vary depending on your specific situation.

More Info

  1. Is healthcare free in Denmark?

While healthcare in Denmark is heavily subsidized by the government, it is not entirely free. Patients may have to pay for certain medical procedures or treatments, but the costs are significantly lower than in many other countries.

  1. How do I open a bank account in Denmark?

To open a bank account in Denmark, you will typically need a CPR number (social security number) and proof of address. It is recommended to research different banks and their requirements beforehand.

  1. What is the cost of living like in Denmark?

The cost of living in Denmark can be quite high, particularly in cities such as Copenhagen. However, salaries are also generally higher than in many other countries, and the standard of living is considered to be excellent.

  1. What is the weather like in Denmark?

Denmark has a temperate maritime climate, which means mild winters and cool summers. However, weather patterns can be unpredictable and can vary significantly from year to year.

  1. What is the education system like in Denmark?

The education system in Denmark is highly regarded and focuses on developing critical thinking and independent learning skills. Education is free for all citizens and permanent residents, and international students can also receive funding for their studies.
